06 / Smile geometry

Volatility smiles, skews and economic interpretation

A smile is the implied-volatility curve across strikes at one expiry. A symmetric smile has higher volatility in both wings than near its center. A skew is an asymmetric pattern; a downward equity-style skew often assigns higher implied volatility to lower strikes. These names describe geometry and do not establish a universal mechanism.

Level, slope and curvature

The level measures the magnitude of implied volatility near a reference strike. The local skew is a first derivative, such as \(\partial_k\sigma(0,T)\). Smile curvature is a second derivative. These are coordinate dependent: derivatives with respect to strike, log-moneyness and delta have different scales.

\[ \frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial k}=\frac{w_k}{2\sqrt{Tw}},\qquad \frac{\partial^2\sigma}{\partial k^2} =\frac{w_{kk}}{2\sqrt{Tw}}-\frac{w_k^2}{4\sqrt T\,w^{3/2}}. \]

A positive curvature in total variance need not imply the same sign in volatility because the square root contributes a negative term. Likewise, a smooth convex-looking volatility plot does not directly establish convexity of call prices. The latter is the economically relevant butterfly condition.

Possible economic drivers

Asymmetric return distributions, price–volatility dependence, jumps, demand for protection, funding constraints and the pricing of volatility risk can all affect a smile. A downturn may increase leverage ratios or coincide with more uncertainty, motivating models with negative price–variance correlation. The same observed skew can nevertheless be generated by several different dynamic models.

Demand for downside protection can influence prices relative to a frictionless model. It does not mean that low-strike implied volatility equals a literal physical probability of a crash. The premium combines payoff magnitude, discounting, risk-neutral weighting and the distribution of terminal prices. Extracting physical probabilities requires further assumptions or data.

Risk reversals and butterflies as quote summaries

A risk reversal is often the difference between call and put IV at matched delta magnitudes. A quoted volatility butterfly is often the average of wing IVs minus ATM IV. Exact definitions vary by market and may involve premium-neutral packages or broker-specific conventions. These quotations are not identical to the payoff-based strike butterfly used to test price convexity.

Write the quotation formula and delta convention before reconstructing a smile from ATM, risk-reversal and butterfly inputs. Inferring wing strikes with one convention and valuing them with another creates a systematic calibration error. Quote summaries are convenient sparse observations, not a complete arbitrage constraint system.

Comparing smiles through time

Fixing absolute strike, log-moneyness or delta leads to different time series when the underlying moves. A decline in spot can move a fixed strike from one part of the smile to another even if the surface in log-moneyness coordinates is unchanged. State the reference coordinate when describing a “volatility increase.”

The laboratory's asymmetry parameter changes a synthetic family of smiles. It is a shape parameter of the selected representation. Interpreting it as the directly observed correlation of price and physical volatility would require a dynamic model and an estimation procedure that are absent from this static example.

07 / Expiry dependence

Maturity structure and forward variance

Annualized implied volatility can increase or decrease with maturity. Short-dated options may concentrate exposure to an imminent event, while long-dated prices average uncertainty over a larger interval. Calendar consistency therefore cannot be tested by requiring volatility itself to increase.

In a deterministic time-dependent volatility model with no smile, the Black total variance equals integrated instantaneous variance. This special case gives a useful interpretation of maturity interpolation:

\[ w(T)=\int_0^T v(t)\,dt,\qquad \sigma_{\mathrm{imp}}(T)^2=\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T v(t)\,dt,\qquad v(T)=\frac{dw(T)}{dT}. \]

For two maturities, the implied forward variance budget in that model is:

\[ v_{T_1,T_2}=\frac{w(T_2)-w(T_1)}{T_2-T_1},\qquad T_2>T_1. \]

If six-month volatility is 30% and one-year volatility is 25%, total variances are 0.045 and 0.0625. The later half-year variance rate is 0.035, with square root approximately 18.71%. Falling annualized volatility is entirely compatible with positive incremental variance. If the one-year IV were 18%, total variance would instead be 0.0324, below 0.045.

{{figure:term}}

A distinction for surfaces with a smile

At a fixed log-moneyness, an increasing \(w(k,T)\) is the appropriate calendar restriction in the deterministic proportional-carry setting developed below. Its maturity derivative should not generally be called the risk-neutral expected instantaneous variance of the underlying. A single smile point is a contract-level Black equivalent, while variance-swap expectations aggregate option prices across strikes.

ATM total variance is a convenient term-structure input for SSVI. The reference example uses a positive exponentially relaxing variance rate. Its short limit is 24% and its long volatility limit 18%, while total variance remains increasing. These are parameters of a synthetic surface, not estimated market forecasts.

Variance swaps and volatility indices

Under a positive continuous-price framework, a log-contract replication motivates an idealized variance-swap strike expressed using out-of-the-money option prices. With suitable integrability and no jump correction:

\[ K_{\mathrm{var}}\approx \frac{2}{TD(T)} \left[\int_0^{F(T)}\frac{P(K,T)}{K^2}\,dK+ \int_{F(T)}^\infty\frac{C(K,T)}{K^2}\,dK\right]. \]

The continuum replication, a discrete exchange index and an ATM implied volatility are distinct quantities. Real implementations specify strike selection, truncation, settlement and interpolation. Cboe's mathematics methodology provides an example of a defined discrete calculation. This guide does not reproduce that index or its operational rules.

The tails matter because the integral reaches beyond actively quoted strikes. Extrapolation and jumps can change variance-related quantities even when near-ATM fit is excellent. A surface intended only for ordinary vanilla interpolation may need stronger tail validation before being used for variance products.

08 / Market data

Option-chain data and contract conventions

A credible calibration begins with a contract table and synchronized observations. Each row should identify the underlying, expiry timestamp, strike, call or put, exercise style, settlement style, currency, multiplier, premium units and quote timestamp. Store raw bid and ask separately from derived midpoint, forward and IV.

Expiry is a timestamp

Calendar days, trading days and actual year fractions are different conventions. An option expiring in a morning auction and one expiring at a closing print can have different residual lives on the same calendar date. Near expiry, several hours can materially change a recovered IV because Black prices depend on total standard deviation \(\sigma\sqrt T\).

Choose a time-zone-aware valuation timestamp and an explicit year-fraction convention. For the numerical examples, \(T\) is an already specified year fraction and no calendar approximation is hidden in the engine. Real market-data ingestion should preserve the raw timestamps needed to reproduce that conversion.

Price observations and trading activity

A last trade may be stale while the current bid and ask are fresh. Volume records historical activity; open interest records outstanding contracts and does not guarantee a tight executable market at the valuation time. A midpoint is an estimate, not a promise that the full desired trade can execute there.

Underlying prices and option quotes should refer to the same time window. A fast spot move between timestamps can create false parity errors and apparent negative time values. If snapshots are asynchronous, either reconstruct a common-time estimate with an explicit method or retain a timing uncertainty flag.

Corporate actions and special contracts

Splits, special dividends and adjusted deliverables can change strike, multiplier or the underlying basket. A contract that delivers a cash component and a nonstandard number of shares does not fit a simple standard-contract formula without adjustment. Combining adjusted and ordinary series in one strike table may make a sound pricing model look inconsistent.

Settlement against a future, an index opening value or cash spot can require different forward inputs. Futures-style margining can alter the interpretation of discounting. Record the instrument's actual payoff and payment timing rather than assigning a convention solely from its ticker.

Reproducible derived fields

Derived IV should carry the price source, pricing model, forward, discount factor, maturity and solver status used to produce it. A useful status vocabulary distinguishes valid finite root, intrinsic limit, outside-bound quote, low-vega unresolved root and missing input. Replacing all of these states with zero loses information.

Store values in numeric decimal units and format percentages only in the display layer. A model expecting 0.24 but receiving 24 can produce an apparently flat price at the volatility upper limit. Unit validation is part of the calculation, not an optional presentation detail.

The synthetic chain in the worked studies omits exchange microstructure deliberately. It fixes every forward and discount input, making any residual identifiable as a solver or model-fitting issue. A real deployment must add the timestamp, contract and quality controls described here.

09 / Carry inputs

Forward estimation, dividends and put–call parity

Implied volatility is sensitive to the forward used for inversion. A carry error can distort a whole smile in a structured way because it changes intrinsic value and the location of forward ATM. Estimate forwards before interpreting call–put discrepancies as differences in volatility.

\[ F(T)=K+\frac{C(K,T)-P(K,T)}{D(T)}. \]

This identity applies to matched European calls and puts with the same payoff dates and settlement convention. Multiple liquid strike pairs supply several estimates of one expiry forward. Their dispersion is a useful quality diagnostic; it does not justify averaging across incompatible contract types.

Intervals from executable sides

Midpoint parity gives a central estimate. Bid and ask provide an interval because buying the call and selling the put has a different cash flow from the reverse package:

\[ K+\frac{C_{\mathrm{bid}}-P_{\mathrm{ask}}}{D} \le F(T)\le K+\frac{C_{\mathrm{ask}}-P_{\mathrm{bid}}}{D}. \]

Transaction costs, financing and shorting constraints complicate an executable arbitrage interpretation. The interval remains a useful measurement range under the specified frictionless parity framework. If several liquid pairs have nonoverlapping intervals, inspect synchronization and conventions before fitting a smooth curve through them.

{{figure:forward}}

Continuous and discrete distributions

A constant proportional yield gives \(F=S_0e^{(r-q)T}\). Known deterministic cash dividends can instead be represented by subtracting their present values from spot before financing the prepaid forward, under the appropriate trading assumptions. This is a carry calculation; it does not automatically justify treating a discrete-dividend equity as a continuous-yield lognormal process for all option valuations.

Uncertain dividends and stock-borrow costs may be embedded in option-implied forwards. A discrepancy from a simple cash-financing calculation can reflect these risks or market frictions. Label the forward as option implied, model based or directly observed rather than asserting that every difference is a data error.

Sensitivity of recovered IV to the forward

At fixed observed call price, implicit differentiation gives \(d\sigma/dF=-C_F/\operatorname{Vega}\). Since \(C_F=D\Phi(d_1)>0\), raising the assumed forward lowers the recovered call IV. For a put, \(P_F=-D\Phi(-d_1)\), and the sign reverses. A forward error can therefore produce systematic disagreement between call and put IV.

Near intrinsic bounds, the wrong forward may eliminate the finite root entirely. A solver that simply clips such quotes to a chosen volatility can hide the original carry problem. Propagate the invalid-input status to the calibration stage and retain the price row for investigation.

Across expiries, forward interpolation should be coherent with the carry model and relevant event dates. A smooth implied-volatility term structure cannot compensate for a discontinuity introduced by an incorrectly timed dividend. Validate the forward curve separately from the volatility surface.

10 / Observation quality

Quote filtering, bid–ask intervals and uncertainty

Cleaning is a documented estimation policy. Reject missing or nonfinite prices, crossed markets, incompatible contracts and premiums outside valid price bounds under the chosen inputs. Flag stale quotes and low-vega observations. Preserve the original rows and the reason for each decision so that a changed filtering policy can be audited.

Price intervals become volatility intervals

Because Black price is monotone in volatility, valid bid and ask premiums invert into an ordered interval. The midpoint premium's IV generally differs from the average of bid IV and ask IV because inversion is nonlinear. Use the actual convention intended by the calibration objective.

{{figure:quotes}}

If one endpoint lies below intrinsic value, a complete finite IV interval cannot be reported without qualification. Clipping that endpoint to intrinsic may be a documented measurement rule, but it changes the data and should be identified. A zero bid in a far wing can indicate insufficient price resolution, rather than a precise zero volatility.

Weighting observations

A simple premium objective is weighted least squares. An observation's width can inform its weight, but a tiny displayed spread is not necessarily trustworthy if the quote is stale. Combine spread, timestamp and contract quality rather than using spread as the sole definition of reliability.

\[ L_{\mathrm{interval}}(p)=\sum_i \frac{\left[\max(C_i^{\mathrm{bid}}-C_i(p),\,0,\,C_i(p)-C_i^{\mathrm{ask}})\right]^2}{s_i^2}. \]

Here \(C_i(p)\) is the fitted model premium and \(s_i>0\) a chosen scale. The loss is zero when the model lies inside the observed market interval. This can avoid chasing arbitrary midpoint noise, while a separate regularization term chooses among many interval-consistent surfaces.

Outliers and robust losses

A robust loss can reduce the influence of unusual residuals, but an outlier may be a genuine event-sensitive contract or a signal of an incorrect forward. Inspect systematic patterns. An entire expiry shifted in one direction suggests a common input issue more readily than seventeen independent anomalous quotes.

Minimum vega thresholds should depend on premium precision and the intended use. A far-wing quote may be too weak to identify IV precisely yet still constrain a tail premium or a digital payoff. Excluding it from a point-IV fit does not mean it contains no economic information.

Measurement uncertainty versus model uncertainty

Bid–ask width describes a market interval under the snapshot. Sampling noise and stale observations add measurement uncertainty. Functional-form choice, missing wings, future smile dynamics and uncertain carry add model uncertainty. A parameter covariance matrix from a local optimizer addresses only part of this picture.

Resample plausible quotes within their intervals, alter quality thresholds and refit from multiple initial points. Compare the resulting prices and derivatives. A small midpoint RMSE can coexist with unstable local variance because differentiation amplifies noise. The target application determines which outputs need the strongest uncertainty assessment.
